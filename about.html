Our story

We wanted to build differently.

Galileo began with Servo and a different view of how the work could be done. We wanted to use AI to help a small European team take on more engineering without handing over the decisions, the testing, or the responsibility.

Servo → Galileo

The route from a shared foundation to our own project.

Galileo did not begin from an empty repository. It began from browser work derived from Servo, with a clear understanding of what we inherited and what we would have to build ourselves.

  1. 01

    The foundation

    Servo gave us a serious place to start.

    Servo supplies the Rust browser-engine foundation: rendering, layout, and a large body of web-platform work. Galileo inherits that work, keeps its origin visible, and adds the integration needed around it.

  2. 02

    The boundary

    Servo drew a clear line around AI-generated contributions.

    Servo’s contribution policy says contributions must not include content generated by large language models or other probabilistic tools. The rule covers code, documentation, pull requests, issues, and comments. We respect that policy and do not present Galileo’s AI-assisted work as upstream Servo work.

    Read Servo’s official AI policy ↗
  3. 03

    The decision

    We chose to continue independently.

    Rather than ask Servo to change its rules, we took responsibility for a separate project. Galileo is where we can test a different model: AI helps increase the amount of work a small team can attempt, while people remain accountable for architecture, review, verification, security, and every release decision.

  4. 04

    The work now

    One project, with two distinct responsibilities.

    Galileo Engine is the browser-engine and integration layer where compatibility, media, extensions, product plumbing, and their tests are developed. Galileo Browser is the desktop product being built on top of it. The code has moved quickly; readiness still has to be demonstrated.

Human responsibility

AI can carry volume. People still own the engineering.

AI output is not treated as authority. The team chooses the direction, reviews the changes, reproduces failures, runs the tests, and decides what can be merged or released.

The next goal is a genuinely usable alpha. Compatibility, security, performance, packaging, and release readiness still have to be earned—and the Roadmap records that boundary.
